Custom Jewelry OEM: From Your Sketch to a Finished Product

Custom jewelry manufacturing, explained step by step — the real MOQ, timeline, sample policy, and where your costs actually go. Straight from a Panyu-based manufacturer.

What Is Custom Jewelry OEM?

OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) means you supply the design, and the factory manufactures it to your specification. For jewelry, that usually means you send a sketch, photo, or CAD file, and a manufacturer turns it into a finished, sellable piece — with your logo, your packaging, and your brand on it.

Custom jewelry OEM is how most serious jewelry brands start. Instead of reselling the same stock styles every other boutique carries, you create pieces that are uniquely yours. The process is more involved than buying stock, but the margins are higher and the differentiation is real.

The Custom Jewelry Process, Step by Step

Here is the standard five-stage flow, with realistic timelines from a Panyu manufacturer:

Stage What Happens Typical Time
1. Design & CAD You send a sketch, photo, or CAD file. We draft the CAD and confirm dimensions, stones, and plating. 3–5 days
2. Sampling A wax or silver sample is made so you can check the look, weight, and finish before bulk. 5–15 days
3. Production The approved sample goes into casting, stone setting, polishing, and plating. 7–15 days
4. Quality Control Piece-by-piece inspection: weight, finish, plating thickness, and stone security. 1–2 days
5. Packaging & Shipping Logo boxes or paper, then DHL/FedEx or sea freight to your door. 2–7 days

What You Need to Provide

The more complete your brief, the faster and cheaper the process. Have these ready before you inquire:

  • A design reference — a photo, sketch, or CAD file (STL or 3dm files work best).
  • Material — 925 sterling silver, titanium steel, or vermeil (gold-plated silver).
  • Stones (optional) — moissanite, cubic zirconia, freshwater pearl, and more.
  • Plating — rhodium, 14K/18K gold, or rose gold.
  • Your logo or stamp — for branding and hallmarking.
  • Target quantity and sizes — ring sizes especially, if your design includes rings.

MOQ, Timeline, and Costs: The Real Numbers

Custom work has different numbers from stock. Here is what to expect:

Item Realistic Figure
Custom MOQ From 30 pcs per style
Sample fee $15–30/pc (includes CAD; not deducted from the bulk order)
Sampling time 5–15 days
Total production time 15–25 days (CAD + sample + production)
Plating options Rhodium, 14K/18K gold (vermeil), rose gold

Pro tip: Always order one sample before committing to 30 pieces. A $15–30 sample is cheap insurance against producing 30 pieces you don't love — and against discovering a sizing or finish issue after the whole batch is done.

Where Your Custom Costs Actually Go

Understanding the cost breakdown helps you see where money is well spent — and where you can save:

  • CAD & mold setup — a one-time cost per design, spread across your order quantity.
  • Metal weight — silver is priced by gram against the live silver rate, so heavier pieces cost more.
  • Stones — moissanite, cubic zirconia, and pearl costs vary widely by size and grade.
  • Plating — rhodium is the most expensive option (rhodium can run 5–10x the price of gold); gold plating is priced by micron thickness.
  • Labor — casting, stone setting, and polishing are skilled work and a large share of cost.
  • Packaging — custom logo boxes usually start from around 100 pieces.

Common OEM Mistakes to Avoid

These are the mistakes we see buyers make most often — and they are all avoidable:

  • Sending a photo without any size reference — proportions come out wrong. Add a coin or ruler in the photo, or state the dimensions.
  • Asking to copy a branded design — we don't copy trademarks or registered shapes (VCA four-leaf, designer logos, etc.). Send an original or a modified design instead.
  • Skipping the sample to save $20 — then approving 30 pieces you never saw. This is the most expensive $20 you'll ever "save."
  • Not confirming plating thickness — thin plating tarnishes fast and hurts your brand reputation.
  • Ordering 30 pcs of an untested design — start with stock or mixed styles to test the market first, then go custom once demand is proven.

Questions Buyers Ask Most About Custom OEM

What is the MOQ for custom jewelry? From 30 pieces per style. For stock styles it's lower (from 10 pieces), and mixed-style orders are usually accepted.

How long does custom jewelry take? Sampling takes 5–15 days, then production another 7–15 days — roughly 15–25 days from confirmed design to finished order.

Do sample fees count toward my bulk order? Sample fees are charged separately and are not deducted from the bulk order — they cover the CAD work and single-piece production.

Can you make a design from a photo? Yes, in most cases. Send a clear photo with a size reference and material notes, and we'll quote it. If it's a trademarked or registered design, we'll ask you to provide an original.
